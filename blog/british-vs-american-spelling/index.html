British vs American Spelling: The Differences and the Rules

Colour or color? Centre or center? Every common British vs American spelling difference explained with simple rules, tables and memory tricks — plus which spellings count in Scrabble.

14 November 2026 | 7 min read | By WordFindLab
Spelling guide

Seven simple patterns cover most differences between British and American spelling, from colour to travelled.

British and American English share almost all of their spellings, but a small set of patterns causes most of the differences: -our and -or, -re and -er, -ise and -ize, doubled L, -ence and -ense, and -ogue and -og. Learn the seven patterns below and you can spell confidently for readers on either side of the Atlantic — and know which spelling your child’s school or your spell-checker expects.

1. -our vs -or

British English keeps the U in many words that came from French; American English dropped it after Noah Webster’s 1828 dictionary popularised the shorter forms.

British (UK)American (US)
colourcolor
favouritefavorite
neighbourneighbor
honourhonor
humourhumor
behaviourbehavior
flavourflavor

Watch out: the U is often dropped in British English too before some endings, so it is humorous and honorary in both, but colourful and honourable in the UK.

2. -re vs -er

British (UK)American (US)
centrecenter
theatretheater
metremeter
litreliter
fibrefiber

In British English a meter is a measuring device (a gas meter) and a metre is 100 centimetres. American English uses meter for both.

3. -ise vs -ize, and -yse vs -yze

American English uses -ize. British English accepts both: most UK newspapers and schools use -ise, while Oxford University Press uses -ize (“Oxford spelling”). The National Curriculum in England uses -ise, for example recognise and criticise on the Year 5 and 6 list.

British (UK)American (US)
organise / organizeorganize
realise / realizerealize
apologise / apologizeapologize
recognise / recognizerecognize
analyseanalyze
paralyseparalyze

Note that analyse and paralyse are always -yse in British English, even in Oxford spelling.

4. Doubling the L

When adding -ed, -ing or -er to a word ending in a vowel plus L, British English doubles the L whatever the stress; American English usually doubles it only when the last syllable is stressed.

British (UK)American (US)
travelled, travellingtraveled, traveling
cancelled, cancellingcanceled, canceling
jewelleryjewelry
woollenwoolen

It can go the other way, too: British enrol, fulfil and skilful have one L where American English writes enroll, fulfill and skillful.

5. -ence vs -ense, and practise vs practice

British (UK)American (US)
defencedefense
offenceoffense
licence (noun), license (verb)license (noun and verb)
practice (noun), practise (verb)practice (noun and verb)

A memory trick for British English: advice (noun) and advise (verb) sound different, and practice/practise and licence/license follow the same C-for-noun, S-for-verb pattern. “I go to football practice so I can practise my shooting.”

6. -ogue vs -og

British (UK)American (US)
cataloguecatalog
dialoguedialog (dialogue is also common)
analogueanalog

Two notes: British English writes program for computer software, and both spellings of judgement/judgment are used in the UK, with judgment preferred in legal writing.

Word games: which spelling counts?

Collins Scrabble Words, the list used in UK clubs and most international play, accepts both British and American spellings of common words, so COLOUR and COLOR are both playable. If you are not sure whether a word is valid in your game, check it in our Scrabble word finder or dictionary. Children in England are taught British spellings. The statutory lists include centre and favourite (see our Year 3 and 4 list) and neighbour, marvellous and programme (see the Year 5 and 6 list).

Frequently asked questions

Why do British and American spellings differ?

Many American spellings were popularised by Noah Webster, whose 1828 American Dictionary of the English Language used shorter forms such as color, center and traveled. British English kept spellings closer to their French or Latin origins.

Is -ize wrong in British English?

No. Both -ise and -ize are correct in British English. Most UK schools and newspapers use -ise, while Oxford University Press uses -ize. Words such as analyse and paralyse are always -yse in British English.

What is the difference between practice and practise?

In British English, practice is the noun and practise is the verb: football practice, but practise the piano. American English uses practice for both.

Is grey or gray correct?

Both are correct. Grey is the usual British spelling and gray is the usual American spelling.

Do children in England learn British or American spellings?

British spellings. The National Curriculum in England uses British English, so words such as centre, favourite, neighbour and recognise appear in their British forms on the statutory word lists.

Are British spellings allowed in Scrabble?

In Collins Scrabble Words, used in the UK and most international play, both British and American spellings of common words are valid. Check any word in our Scrabble word finder.
