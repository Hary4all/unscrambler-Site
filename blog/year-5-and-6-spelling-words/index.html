These words come from the statutory word list in English Appendix 1 of the National Curriculum in England (Department for Education, 2013). The curriculum describes the lists as a mixture of words pupils use often in their writing and words they often misspell. Your child’s school may teach them in its own order. The trickiest words and how to remember them

  • accommodate — double C and double M: it has room for two of each.
  • committee — double M, double T, double E.
  • embarrass — double R and double S. Compare harass, which has only one R.
  • necessary — one C, two Ss: “one collar, two sleeves”.
  • rhythm and rhyme — both start RH. Rhythm has no vowel except the Y.
  • mischievous — only three syllables: mis-chie-vous. There is no “i” before -ous.
  • lightning — the flash in a storm has no E. “Lightening” means making something lighter.
  • yacht, muscle, soldier and twelfth — letters that hide: CH is silent in yacht, C is silent in muscle, DI in soldier sounds like “j”, and the F in twelfth is easy to miss.
  • conscience and conscious — both contain SCI. Conscience is your sense of right and wrong; conscious means awake or aware.

British spellings to watch

Several words on this list are spelled differently in American English: criticise and recognise (US: criticize, recognize), marvellous (US: marvelous), neighbour (US: neighbor) and programme (US: program). Children in England are taught the British forms, so set spell-checkers to English (UK). See all the patterns in British vs American spelling.

Easy ways to practise

  • Five a week: 100 words at five a week takes 20 weeks, so starting in Year 5 leaves plenty of time to revisit tricky ones in Year 6.
  • Spot the pattern: group words by trap — double letters (accommodate, committee, embarrass), silent letters (yacht, muscle, rhythm), -ise endings (criticise, recognise).
  • Make a word search: put this week’s words into the printable word search maker.
  • Unscramble challenge: try VEHICLE or SYMBOL in our unscrambler and see what other words hide inside.
  • Check meanings: look up words like hindrance, prejudice or sufficient in the dictionary and use each in a sentence.
  • Five-minute game: end with our free spelling games for kids.

Frequently asked questions

How many words are on the Year 5 and 6 spelling list?

There are 100 entries. A few include related forms, such as equip (equipped, equipment), immediate(ly) and sincere(ly).

Is the Year 5 and 6 word list statutory?

Yes. English Appendix 1 of the National Curriculum in England says the word lists for Years 3 and 4 and Years 5 and 6 are statutory, so schools following the National Curriculum teach them by the end of Year 6.

What is the hardest word on the Year 5 and 6 list?

Many children find accommodate, embarrass, mischievous, rhythm, necessary and conscience the hardest because of double letters, silent letters or unusual vowel patterns.

Why do these lists use British spellings?

The National Curriculum in England uses British English, so words such as recognise, neighbour, marvellous and programme appear in their British forms.

Are these words in the Year 6 SATs?

The Key Stage 2 grammar, punctuation and spelling test includes a spelling paper. The statutory list is part of what Year 6 pupils are expected to know, but the test is not limited to these 100 words.
