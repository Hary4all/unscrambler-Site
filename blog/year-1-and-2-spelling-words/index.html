Year 1 and 2 Common Exception Words: The Full KS1 List

All 45 Year 1 and 64 Year 2 common exception words from the National Curriculum in England, printable, with the tricky patterns explained and quick ways to practise at home.

Common exception words are the everyday words children can’t spell just by sounding out — said, friend, could and once. The National Curriculum in England lists 45 of them for Year 1 and 64 for Year 2. Here are both lists, ready to print, followed by why each group is tricky and short, fun ways to practise at home.

Why these words are tricky

  • Odd vowel sounds: said, says, friend, again (the ai and ie sound like a short E) and one, once (which start with a W sound).
  • O that sounds like U: love, come, some and money. Children who sound out “l-o-v” will write luv.
  • -ind, -ild and -old families: find, kind, mind, behind; child, wild; old, cold, gold, hold, told. The I and O say their names, so teach them as a set.
  • EA saying “ay”: great, break and steak.
  • Long “ah” A: after, fast, last, past, father, class, grass, pass, plant, path and bath are said with a long “ah” in many southern accents, so children want to write ar.
  • Silent letters: climb (silent B), hour (silent H), could, should, would (silent L), half (silent L), Christmas (silent T and CH saying K), who and whole (silent W).
  • OO and OU surprises: door, floor and poor; move, prove and improve; put, push, pull and full (a short OO sound written with U).

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Three a day: 109 words sounds like a lot, but three words on school days covers both lists in under two terms.
  • Teach families together: learn could, should and would on the same day, then old, cold, gold, hold and told.
  • Look, say, cover, write, check: the method most UK primary schools use.
  • Spot them in books: words like the, said and was appear on almost every page of a reading book. Ask your child to find five.
  • Make a word search: type this week’s words into our printable word search maker, or play the online word search for kids.
  • Unscramble race: mix up the letters of a word such as FRIEND and see how fast your child finds it.
  • Five-minute game: finish with a round of our spelling games for kids.

Frequently asked questions

How many common exception words are there in Year 1 and Year 2?

There are 45 common exception words listed for Year 1 and 64 for Year 2, so 109 in total across Key Stage 1.

What is a common exception word?

A common exception word is a word children meet often but cannot spell by sounding out with the phonics they have learnt so far, either because part of it is spelled in an unusual way (said, friend, once) or because it uses a spelling pattern taught later.

Where do the Year 1 and Year 2 lists come from?

They are listed in the spelling section of English Appendix 1 of the National Curriculum in England (Department for Education, 2013), in the statutory requirements for Year 1 and Year 2.

Why are words like fast, grass and path on the Year 2 list?

In many accents, especially in the south of England, the A in these words is said as a long “ah”, so children may expect to write ar. Children in other areas say a short A, but the words are taught for everyone.

Is Christmas on the Year 2 list?

Yes. Christmas is one of the 64 Year 2 common exception words, along with Mr, Mrs and parents. The silent T and the capital letter make it a good one to practise in December.

What comes after the Year 1 and 2 words?

In Year 3 and Year 4 children move on to the statutory word list of 100 words, such as believe, February and separate. See our Year 3 and 4 spelling list.
