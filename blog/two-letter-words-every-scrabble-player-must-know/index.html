The 101 Two-Letter Words Every Scrabble Player Must Memorize

Knowing these words separates recreational players from competitive ones. Two-letter words are the skeleton key to premium squares.

⚠ The average casual Scrabble player knows about 25 two-letter words. Tournament players know all 101. That gap alone is worth an estimated 40–60 points per game.

Two-letter words are the most high-leverage thing you can memorize in Scrabble. They let you squeeze plays into tight boards, create parallel words that score twice, hook onto existing words, and most importantly — reach the triple-word squares that recreational players leave untouched.

Why 2-Letter Words Are Worth More Than Long Words

A 7-letter bingo scores 50 bonus points but requires the perfect rack and an open board. A well-placed 2-letter word on a double-word square takes three seconds to spot and can score 20–30 points on its own — plus create a parallel word alongside it for another 10–20.

The real power is in parallel plays. When you play a word alongside an existing word, every overlap creates a new 2-letter word. Knowing all valid combinations means you can evaluate whether a parallel is legal in seconds rather than guessing.

Pro Tip

Before playing any word, scan the adjacent squares. If you can form a parallel word that runs alongside an existing word, you often double your score for free — but only if the crossings are valid 2-letter words.

Common Mistake

Many players try BI, but forget that BU, BO, and BE are also valid. Similarly, OI is valid (an exclamation) but OA, OB, and OC are not. Don't assume — memorize the full list.

How to Learn All 101: A Memory System That Works

Trying to memorize 101 words as a random list fails. Here's the approach competitive players actually use:

  1. Start with the 20 most-useful words: AA, AB, AE, AI, OE, OI, QI, ZA, XI, XU, EX, OX, AX, JO, KA, KI, MU, NU, UT, OM. These give the highest return on memory investment.
  2. Learn the vowel pairs: AA (type of lava), AE (one, Scottish), AI (three-toed sloth), OE (whirlwind), OI (exclamation). These solve the two-vowel rack nightmare.
  3. Learn the Q, Z, X, J words as a group: QI, ZA, ZIT, XI, XU, EX, OX, AX, JO. When you draw these tiles, you immediately know your 2-letter escape routes.
  4. Add one letter's words per day: Spend 5 minutes with flashcards on Monday for all A-words, Tuesday for B-words. You cover all 101 in three weeks.
  5. Play a test game where you challenge yourself: Every turn, before playing, identify every valid 2-letter parallel you could make. Score only those plays for five games.
Memory Trick

For the weird ones: AA is a type of rough lava (Hawaii), AE means "one" in Scottish dialect, KA is the Egyptian spirit double, ZA is slang for pizza, and OI is the British punk exclamation. Attach a vivid image to each and they stick.

Key Takeaway

You don't need to memorize all 101 at once. The first 20 vowel-heavy and high-tile words give you 80% of the competitive benefit. Add 5 new words per week and you'll have the full list in five months — just in time to start winning more games.

Using 2-Letter Words as Hooks

A hook is a letter added to an existing word to make a new word. Two-letter words are ideal hooks because they can be played perpendicular to any word already on the board, and the crossing letter often forms a new 2-letter word simultaneously.

Example: If STONE is on the board, playing MA perpendicular through the S creates MA + S = MAS (valid) and adds MA's base score. Playing ZA perpendicular through the A creates ZA + A = ZAA (not valid) — so you need to know these connections cold.
