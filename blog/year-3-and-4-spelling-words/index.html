Here is the complete list in A–Z order, ready to print, followed by the patterns that catch children out and short, fun ways to practise at home. Some entries include a related form, such as busy/business or occasion(ally), and children learn both.

The full Year 3 and 4 word list (A–Z)

These words come from the statutory word list in English Appendix 1 of the National Curriculum in England (Department for Education, 2013). The curriculum describes the lists as a mixture of words pupils use often in their writing and words they often misspell. Your child’s school may teach them in its own order. Tricky patterns in the Year 3 and 4 list

  • Silent letters: answer (silent W), island (silent S), knowledge (silent K), build, guard and guide (silent U), reign (silent G).
  • The OUGH family: enough, though, although, thought and through look alike but all sound different. See more words containing OUGH.
  • EIGH words: eight, eighth, height and weight. Height is the odd one out: it rhymes with “kite”. More words containing EIGH.
  • Double letters: address, appear, arrive, different, difficult, disappear, grammar, opposite, possess, pressure, suppose and occasion.
  • Sound-alike pairs: heard/herd, weight/wait, reign/rain, eight/ate and breath (a noun, short E) versus breathe (a verb, long E).
  • Unstressed vowels: separate (sep-A-rate), library (two Rs), February (don’t forget the first R), probably and interest.

British spellings to watch

Two words on this list are spelled differently in American English: centre (US: center) and favourite (US: favorite). Spell-checkers set to US English will flag the British forms, so check that your device is set to English (UK) when children type their spellings. More in our guide to British vs American spelling.

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Five a week: learning five words a week covers all 100 in 20 weeks — roughly two school terms.
  • Look, say, cover, write, check: the classic method most UK primary schools use.
  • Make a word search: type this week’s five words into our printable word search maker, or play the online word search for kids.
  • Unscramble race: mix up the letters of a word (for example ISLAND) and time how fast your child finds it.
  • Use them in sentences: one silly sentence with three list words is more memorable than ten copies of each word.
  • Five-minute game: finish with a round of our spelling games for kids.

Frequently asked questions

How many words are on the Year 3 and 4 spelling list?

There are 100 entries. A few include a related form, such as busy/business, eight/eighth and woman/women, so children actually practise a few more than 100 spellings.

Is the Year 3 and 4 word list statutory?

Yes. English Appendix 1 of the National Curriculum in England says the word lists for Years 3 and 4 and Years 5 and 6 are statutory. Schools in England following the National Curriculum teach them by the end of Year 4.

What are the hardest words on the Year 3 and 4 list?

Children often find February, separate, peculiar, knowledge, reign, height, library, naughty and the OUGH words (though, thought, through, enough) the hardest.

Do children need to know the Year 1 and 2 words first?

Yes. The Year 3 and 4 list builds on the common exception words from Key Stage 1, such as said, because and friend. See the full Year 1 and 2 common exception words.

How should we practise the list at home?

Little and often works best: about five words a week, using look, say, cover, write, check, plus games such as word searches and unscrambling.
